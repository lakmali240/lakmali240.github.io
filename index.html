About

Data-efficient machine learning, from research to products

I'm a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of California, Davis, working with Prof. Chen-Nee Chuah (Electrical & Computer Engineering) and Prof. Brittany N. Dugger (Pathology & Laboratory Medicine) on foundation models and agentic vision–language systems for gigapixel pathology images.

I earned my Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Kentucky in the Multimedia Information Analysis Lab with Prof. Sen-ching Samson Cheung. My research focuses on data-efficient and cost-effective machine learning across medical imaging, computational pathology, dermoscopy, and large-scale product data: active learning, self-supervised learning, and foundation-model methods that keep performance high under severe class imbalance, few labels, and gigapixel inputs.

Beyond academia, I have twice interned as an Applied Scientist at Amazon, building multimodal classification and generative AI systems, and spent three years as an Applications Engineer II at Synopsys validating industry-leading EDA verification tools. I hold a B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering from the University of Peradeniya, Sri Lanka.

Amazon · Merch on Demand · SeattleMay – Aug 2025

Applied Scientist Intern: Generative AI pipeline

Problem
Show products in realistic, context-aware scenes without manual design work.
Approach
An end-to-end pipeline using diffusion models (FLUX.1, CatVTON-Flux) for image generation, LoRA-based refinement for style consistency, and LLM-based quality evaluation that scores every output.
Result
Automated generation and quality control, deployed on multi-GPU cloud infrastructure with AWS SageMaker.

Amazon · Merch on Demand · SeattleJun – Aug 2024

Applied Scientist Intern: Multimodal classification

Problem
Categorize large-scale product data by theme, where keywords alone miss what a product is about.
Approach
A multi-modal, multi-label classifier combining image and text features (CLIP), trained through an automated active-learning loop with LLM-based pseudo-labeling.
Result
Significantly outperformed keyword-based baselines in theme recognition accuracy.
